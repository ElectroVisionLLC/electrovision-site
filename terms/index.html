Estimates, AI limitations, and your responsibility to verify

Everything the App produces is an estimate

The App's appliance identifications, wattage figures, kilowatt-hour projections, cost calculations, efficiency ratings, grades, and recommendations are estimates. They are not measurements of your actual energy consumption. Only your utility meter and your utility bill record what you actually used.

Energy models necessarily rely on assumptions — typical wattages for a class of appliance, average usage patterns, regional rates — and on the accuracy of the information you enter. Changing any of those inputs changes the output.

The App uses artificial intelligence, which can be wrong

Appliance identification, meter reading, and bill reading are performed by artificial intelligence systems. You should understand the following about how such systems behave:

  • They can hallucinate — that is, generate information that is plausible-sounding but false, including specifications, model names, or figures that do not exist.
  • They can misidentify objects, reporting an appliance that is not present or missing one that is.
  • They can misread numbers on a meter or a utility bill, which will in turn distort every calculation derived from that reading.
  • They express confidence that does not reliably correspond to correctness. A high confidence score is not proof that an answer is right.

These are inherent limitations of the underlying technology. We work to reduce them, but we cannot eliminate them, and we do not warrant that the App's output is accurate.

You are responsible for verifying accuracy

It is your responsibility to check anything the App tells you before relying on it. Every figure in the App is editable precisely so that you can correct it. Compare the App's estimates against your actual utility bills. If a number looks wrong, it may well be wrong.
